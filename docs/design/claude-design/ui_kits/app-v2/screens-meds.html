MT · Meds timeline

Replaces Upcoming and History (briefs/meds-timeline.md). The sub-tabs become Timeline · Schedule · Stock. One row per (date, time) group, read from the dose engine, so it shows the same dose as Telegram and the notifications.

Review of the brief

  • The "Taken" block also holds skipped doses. Labelling it "Taken" makes a skipped dose look taken at a glance. It's labelled Done, and each row has its own chip (Taken 08:24 / Skipped).
  • A group can split across blocks. If two of the three 08:20 meds are taken and one isn't, that's two rows: one in Pending and one in Done, each acting only on its own occIds. Drawn in MT1.
  • Same time, different zone = different groups. 08:20 Berlin and 08:20 New York never merge, even when they share a local time label (MT2).
  • After a long trip, Today's Pending can push Next off screen. Next stays first, as in the sketch, so the sun is always visible. Dated pending rows are grouped under a date subhead in Pending, and the per-day bulk action appears there too, not only in the earlier day (MT4).
  • Offline writes aren't specified. Drawn as: taps queue on the device and sync later, because the dose id makes duplicates impossible. The outdated-app state blocks writes instead (MT5).
  • A valid reminder tap (still matches a dose) should open M2 directly, not the timeline. Only out-of-date taps land on MT6. Not drawn.
  • No "missed". M1's "08:20 · missed today" (danger) now reads Pending, in the pending tone.

Rules on this page

  • The sun is the Take on the soonest Next group, and nowhere else. Pending Take is a raised button; Skip is ghost. A row tap opens the MT3 sheet (kit rule 3).
  • State is shown by the row's lead and chip together: upcoming (neutral), due/snoozed (pending), taken (ok), skipped (stale, dashed), flagged (warn). The time alone never carries state.
  • A zone appears only when it differs from the current one: "08:20 · Berlin", with the local equivalent as meta.
  • New in v2.2: .wg-row__lead--warn and --stale, completing the lead set for the five status families.
