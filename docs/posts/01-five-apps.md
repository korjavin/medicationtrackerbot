## LinkedIn (389 words)

My blood pressure got worse over one month last year, and I couldn't tell you why, because the answer was sitting in five apps that don't talk to each other.

The pharmacy app knew what I was taking and when I refilled it. The scale posted my weight to a cloud in a country I've never visited. My watch had the sleep. My meals were in a notes file I'd stopped updating by week three. The readings themselves came from a cuff that emails you an export with the dates in a format nothing else reads.

Five tidy graphs, and the question I had ran through all of them. Was I sleeping worse before the numbers moved, or after? Had the workouts stopped first? Did a dose change land in the same week?

Nobody is paid to answer that. Each of those apps is paid to keep me inside it, and a clean way out is the last thing it will ship. So the export stays broken and the whole picture never leaves the building.

There's a second reason I never had the whole picture, and that one is mine. I don't type everything in. Missed doses, the drinking, the symptom you'd rather not name, the week you gave up. Anyone who has ever softened a number for a doctor knows the feeling.

A log a company can read gets softened the same way.

A year of medications lined up against weight, sleep and blood pressure says what I'm treated for and whether it's working. I wouldn't hand that to my closest friend, and most of it is sitting on machines I've never seen, readable by people I'll never meet.

So there are two things on the shelf: keep it all in one place and let a company read it, or keep it private and keep it useless.

I spent last year building the third one. It's called myhealthbot.ai. It holds all of it, and the machine it lives on cannot read a line of mine. Last month it answered my question out of seven months of my own records.

The apps can show me a graph because that keeps me coming back. Handing me the whole usable record would let me leave.

That's not an accident.

P.S. Next post: whose machine holds the real copy, and what happens to my record if every server on earth goes dark.
